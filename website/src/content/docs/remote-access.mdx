Claude often works for minutes and then stops to ask before it runs a command, or to ask you a question. With Remote you see that on your phone and answer it there, from the couch, a meeting or the train.

On your phone you can:

- see your sessions with their status and each Claude profile's usage, and answer what Claude asks right from the list;
- switch between sessions from a drawer, with one tap;
- read a session's conversation as it happens, with Claude's answers formatted and its tool runs folded into one line;
- allow or deny what Claude asks, answer its questions, and start or keep working on a plan;
- send a prompt, or add one to Up next while Claude works, and remove it again;
- see what a finished session did, look at its changes and commit them;
- stop Claude's turn, mark a session as read or archive it, with a swipe;
- start a new session in one of the projects you added in Switchboard, with that project's defaults or your own choice of Claude profile, model, effort, mode, worktree and branch;
- get a notification when a session needs you or finishes.

Everything else stays on your computer: the terminal, pushing and pull requests, memory, settings, managing profiles and file attachments.

## How it works

Switchboard on your computer listens on `127.0.0.1`, port 47615 unless you change it under **Advanced** → **Port** in Settings → Remote. Only your computer can reach that address. A **tunnel** gives it an `https://` address your phone can reach from anywhere. The page on your phone is the remote app at `switchboard.struyfconsulting.com/remote/`, and it connects to your computer through the tunnel.

Only devices you pair can get in. Each one has its own key, every connection checks that key and your computer's, and everything is encrypted from your phone to your computer. The tunnel carries your messages, but it can't read or change them.

## Settings → Remote

<Screenshot name="settings-remote" alt="Settings on Remote: the switch next to the title, a green card that says Running with the address and Pair a device, two paired devices with Rename and Revoke, the connection My own address with Change…, Advanced folded, and Other computers with a field for a link" />

Settings → Remote (<Kbd shortcut="settings" />) shows first what you open it for:

- **The status card** says whether Remote works: **Running** with the address and who is connected, or what is wrong with the fix as a button. **Install Tailscale** or **Use Cloudflare instead** when a tool is missing, **Use port 47616** when another app has the port, **Try again** and **Show details** when it can't start.
- **Paired devices**, each with when it last connected, **Rename** and **Revoke**.
- **Connection**: the tunnel and its address. **Change…** opens the choices, and nothing changes until **Save and restart**.
- **Advanced**: the port, and how Remote keeps others out.

With Remote off and nothing paired, the page shows three steps instead: choose a tunnel, **Turn on Remote**, then pair a device.

## Choose a tunnel

Under **Connection** in Settings → Remote, click **Change…** to choose how your phone reaches this computer. Switchboard starts the tunnel when you turn Remote on and stops it when you turn Remote off or quit.

| Tunnel | Needs | Address |
|---|---|---|
| **Cloudflare quick tunnel** | `cloudflared` installed, no account | Changes each time it starts |
| **Tailscale** | Tailscale on your computer and your phone, HTTPS certificates on for your tailnet | Stays the same |
| **ngrok** | `ngrok` installed and signed in to your account | Stays the same with your ngrok domain |
| **My own address** | A tunnel or proxy you run yourself, forwarding to `http://127.0.0.1` and your port (47615 by default) | Yours |

**Tailscale** is the most private: only your own devices on your tailnet can reach the address. With the others, anyone who has the address can reach Remote, but only paired devices get past it.

### Cloudflare with your own domain

A quick tunnel gets a new address each time it starts. If your domain is on Cloudflare, a named tunnel gives you an address that never changes, such as `https://switchboard.example.com`.

1. Follow [Cloudflare's guide to create a tunnel](https://developers.cloudflare.com/tunnel/get-started/) and run it on your computer.
2. Add a public hostname to the tunnel with your domain. Set its service to `http://127.0.0.1:47615`, or your own port if you changed it.
3. In **Settings → Remote**, choose **My own address** and enter the `https://` address of that hostname.

Don't put a login page, such as Cloudflare Access, in front of it: your phone can't get past it.

<Aside type="caution" title="ngrok's free plan">
ngrok's free plan can show a warning page in front of your address, which a phone can't get past. If your phone can't connect through ngrok, use Cloudflare or Tailscale.
</Aside>

<OnlyOn os="mac">
Install `cloudflared` or `ngrok` with Homebrew (`brew install cloudflared`, `brew install ngrok`). Switchboard finds them in Homebrew's folders and in `/usr/local/bin`. Tailscale's app from the App Store or tailscale.com works as it is.
</OnlyOn>

<OnlyOn os="windows">
Install `cloudflared` or `ngrok` with winget (`winget install Cloudflare.cloudflared`, `winget install ngrok.ngrok`). Switchboard finds them where winget puts them, in Program Files and on your PATH. Tailscale's installer from tailscale.com works as it is.
</OnlyOn>

## Pair a device

<Steps>

1. In **Settings → Remote**, choose a tunnel and click **Turn on Remote**. Wait until the card says **Running**.

2. Click **Pair a device**. Switchboard shows a QR code. It works once, for two minutes.

3. Scan it with your phone's camera. Your phone opens the remote app and asks for a name for the phone.

4. Tap **Pair**. Your phone shows a 6-digit code, and Switchboard asks **Pair *your phone*?** with a code too.

5. Check that both codes are the same, then click **Allow**.

</Steps>

To use Remote in the browser of another computer instead, click **Copy link** under the QR code and open the link there. It pairs the same way and works once, for two minutes, like the code.

Your phone shows your sessions. Add it to your home screen to open it like an app. Next time it connects by itself.

<Aside type="note" title="On an iPhone or iPad">
Switchboard Remote on your Home Screen keeps its own pairing, apart from Safari's, and has no address bar. So pair it from there: after you scan the QR code, don't tap **Pair** in Safari. Tap **Copy link**, then Share and **Add to Home Screen**. Open Switchboard Remote from your Home Screen, tap **Paste** and pair. The link works once, for two minutes: if it runs out, click **Pair a device** again, scan the new code and paste its link.
</Aside>

A link from your computer also works pasted in: in Switchboard Remote without a pairing, paste it under **Link from your computer** and tap **Open link**. This works for a link from **Reconnect a paired device** too.

<Aside type="tip" title="When the address changes">
A Cloudflare quick tunnel, or ngrok without a domain, gets a new address each time it starts. When no paired device has used the new one yet, the card says **Address changed**. Your devices don't have to pair again: click **Reconnect a paired device** and scan that code with one of them. It holds no secret and pairs nothing. The same button is under **Advanced**.
</Aside>

## Change the port

Switchboard listens on port 47615. If another app uses it, the card says so and offers the next free port. To pick one yourself, open **Advanced** in Settings → Remote and change **Port** to a number from 1024 to 65535. Remote restarts on the new port. With **My own address**, point your tunnel at the new port too.

## Paired devices, Rename and Revoke

**Paired devices** in Settings → Remote lists your phones, browsers and other computers, each with when it last connected and **Connected now** while it is. **Rename** changes a device's name in place: <Kbd keys="enter" /> saves, <Kbd keys="escape" /> cancels. **Revoke** asks first, then disconnects the device at once, and it can't connect again until you pair it again. A device that doesn't connect for 30 days is removed. On the phone, **Forget this computer…** in the computer's menu (tap its name at the top) or in the **⋯** menu removes the pairing from the phone.

## Who is connected now

<Screenshot name="connected-devices" alt="The bottom of the sidebar with Connected to this Mac and a green 2: Sam's iPhone and Safari on Studio laptop, each with since when. Next to it, the details of Sam's iPhone: Connected since, Last action, Paired 3 days ago, the address it came through, Remote settings and Revoke…" />

While Remote is on, **<Os mac="Connected to this Mac" windows="Connected to this PC" />** at the bottom of the sidebar lists the phones, browsers and computers connected right now, with since when. It sits right above **Computers**: one is who reaches into this computer, the other is where you reach out. Click the header to fold it, and the gear to open Settings → Remote.

- **Click a device** to see its details: since when it is connected, its last action, such as **Sent a prompt in Needs review sort** or **Answered Allow Bash in tideline**, when it was paired, and the address it came through. Click the session's name to open it. The last action is kept until Switchboard quits; before that, it says **No actions yet**.
- **Revoke** shows when you point at a device or move to it with <Kbd keys="tab" />. It asks first, then disconnects the device at once. It has to pair again to come back. <Kbd keys="escape" /> cancels.
- **A new connection** shows a toast, such as **Sam's iPhone connected**, with **Revoke** in case it wasn't you. It shows once a day per device, and not right after you paired it.
- **Nobody connected**: a quiet line, **Remote on · nobody connected**, says Remote is on. To hide it, turn off **Show in the sidebar when nobody is connected** in Settings → Remote.
- **Remote can't start**: the line turns orange. Click it to see why in Settings → Remote.

With the sidebar collapsed, a phone icon with a green count shows how many devices are connected. Point at it for their names, or click it for the details, with **Revoke…** for each.

## Your sessions

<Screenshot name="remote-phone-first-screen" width={390} alt="The remote app on a phone: Studio Mac in large type at the top, with a chevron, and under it a green dot, Connected · 3 need you. The search and ⋯ buttons on the right. Under it a Profiles card with Personal and Work, each with a 5h and a Week bar, Work at 91% in red. Then Needs you with a question and two prompts with Deny and Allow, and New session at the bottom." />

The app opens on your sessions. This is the app's only home on a phone: everything else is one step away from it. At the top is your computer's name. Tap it for your computer's menu. Under it, one line says how your phone is connected and what is going on, such as **Connected · 2 need you** or **Connected · 1 finished today**. On the right are the magnifier and the **⋯** menu.

When you scroll down, the name moves into the bar at the top, in small type with the dot, and the magnifier and the **⋯** menu stay there.

Tap the magnifier to search the titles, project names and branches. The search field takes the name's place, with **Cancel** next to it. While you search, **All**, **Needs you** and **Working** under the field narrow the list. **Cancel** closes the search and shows your whole list again.

The card under it shows how much of your plan is used: **Usage** with a bar for the 5-hour window and one for the week. With more than one Claude profile it is **Profiles**, with a row for each, its colour and both bars. With three or more, it shows the two closest to a limit and **+1** for the rest. A bar turns orange from 60% and red from 85%. Tap the card for each profile's full card, with when each window resets; tap it again to fold them. The numbers come from your computer: they update when it checks, and each time you come back to the app.

What Claude asks shows next, as cards with a pink frame, the oldest first:

- A command, an edit or a fetch has **Deny** and **Allow** on the card. **Allow** answers it from the list, and the card folds away. **Always allow** isn't on the card: open the session to read what it does.
- A question or a plan is a short row. Tap it to answer in the session.

Tap anywhere else on a card to open its session. If someone answered first, on your computer or another phone, the phone says **Already answered**.

Below the cards are your sessions by group: **Working**, **Today**, **Yesterday** and **Earlier**. Sessions you haven't read have a bold title. With more than one Claude profile, a dot in the profile's colour comes before the project's name, and the session's left edge has that colour too, as in Switchboard's sidebar. **New session** is at the bottom of the screen.

Pull the list down from the top to refresh it: it says **Pull to refresh**, then **Release to refresh**, and shows a spinner until the new list is in.

### Swipe a session

- **Swipe right** to mark it as read, on blue with a check.
- **Swipe left** to archive it.

A short swipe shows the button: tap it, or tap the session to put it back. A long swipe, past most of the row, does it at once. Either way a message says what happened, with **Undo**. A session that needs you doesn't swipe, so you can't dismiss a prompt by accident: open it to answer.

A long press on a session has the same **Mark as read** and **Archive**, and so does a right-click in a browser on a computer. With a screen reader, each session has its own actions button. Undo after **Mark as read** needs this version of Switchboard on your computer.

## Switch sessions

<Screenshot name="remote-phone-drawer" width={390} alt="The sessions drawer over a session: Sessions with the computer chip, a search field, a Home row with Personal 58% and Work 91%, the sessions by group with the open one picked, and New session at the bottom." />

In a session, the button at the top left opens your other sessions in a drawer. Its pink count says how many other sessions need you. You can also swipe from the left edge of the screen to pull the drawer out. Tap a session to switch to it.

The drawer has a search field, **Home**, your sessions by group with the open one picked, and **New session** at the bottom. Tap **Home** to go back to the first screen. With more than one Claude profile, the **Home** row shows how much each profile has used, such as **Work 82%** and **Personal 18%**. With three or more, it shows the one closest to a limit and **+2**.

To close the drawer, tap next to it, swipe it to the left, or use the back gesture. A session you pick takes the place of the one you had open, so the back gesture goes back to your sessions, not through every session you looked at.

## Your computer's menu

<Screenshot name="remote-phone-computer-menu" width={390} alt="The computer's name opened as a menu: Studio Mac, Connected for under a minute · encrypted, then Disconnect, Pair another computer…, and Forget this computer… in red." />

The line under your computer's name has a green dot and **Connected** while your phone is connected. It turns orange with **Reconnecting…** while your phone tries again, red with **Offline** when it stopped trying, and grey with **Disconnected** after you disconnect. In the drawer, and in a wide window, the chip with your computer's name shows the same dot. Tap the name, or the chip, for your computer's menu:

- Its name and how it's connected, such as **Connected for 2h · encrypted**.
- **Disconnect** closes the connection and keeps the pairing. The app says **Disconnected from** your computer, with **Connect**, and shows your last list greyed out. It stays disconnected until you tap **Connect**, also after a reload. When you open the app again later, it connects by itself.
- Your other computers, if you paired more than one, each with how many sessions need you there, or **Offline** or **Disconnected**. Tap one to switch to it.
- **Pair another computer…** pairs this phone with one more computer. See [More than one computer](#more-than-one-computer).
- **Forget this computer…** asks first, then removes the pairing from this phone. Your other computers stay paired, and the app shows one of them. To use Remote with this computer again, pair the phone again.

## More than one computer

Your phone can be paired with several computers, such as a laptop and a desktop. Each one runs Switchboard with Remote on, and you pair the phone with each of them.

1. In the computer's menu (tap its name at the top), tap **Pair another computer…**.
2. On the other computer, open Settings → Remote, turn on **Allow access from my phone** and click **Pair a device**.
3. Scan its QR code with your phone's camera, or click **Copy link** under the code and paste the link on your phone. On an iPhone with Switchboard Remote on the Home Screen, tap **Copy link** on the page that the camera opens, and paste it in the app.

The app shows one computer at a time, but stays connected to all of them. When sessions on another computer need you, a row above your sessions says so, such as **mystic · 2 need you**. Tap it to switch to that computer. You can also switch from the computer's menu, or with **Switch to** in the search (<Kbd keys="mod+k" />) in a wide window.

The count on the drawer button and in the browser tab adds up what needs you on every computer. The app opens on the computer you looked at last. Each computer keeps its own pairing: **Disconnect** and **Forget this computer…** only affect the computer on screen.

## A session

The header shows the session's status, project and branch, and with more than one Claude profile, the profile it runs with, such as **· Work**. The **⋯** menu has **Stop** while Claude works, **Mark as read** and **Archive**. Opening a session marks it as read, as on your computer.

When Claude needs you, the question stays above the message box, so it doesn't scroll away: **Claude needs you**, what it asks, and **Deny**, **Always allow** when it's offered, and **Allow**. The arrow folds it to one line while you read the conversation. With more than one, **1 of 2** and the arrows step through them. While it's open, the message box says **Or tell Claude what to do instead**: what you send there says no, with your message as the reason.

While you type, the keyboard never covers the message box, and the question folds to one line above it, such as **Claude needs you · Allow Bash?**. Tap that line to read it again: the keyboard goes away and the question unfolds.

While Claude works, the end of the conversation shows three dots, how long the turn has taken and what Claude is doing, such as **Thinking** or **Using Bash**. A working session's dot pulses, in the list and in the header.

When Claude's turn ended but a background command or agent it started still runs, the session isn't done yet: Claude carries on when that finishes. The header says **Running in the background**, the end of the conversation says what still runs in green, such as **1 background command running**, and the catch-up waits until it's done. A green pill above the message box, such as **1 background command**, lists each task with how long it has run. Tap **Stop** next to one to stop it; Claude is told it was stopped. Seeing and stopping the tasks needs this version of Switchboard on your computer.

Claude's answers show headings, lists, links, code and tables, also while Claude is still writing them. Code blocks have **Copy**. A run of tool calls is one line, such as **6 steps · edited 3 files, ran 2 commands**: tap it to see each step. The run Claude is working on stays open.

When a session finished, and nothing it started still runs in the background, it starts with its catch-up, as on your computer: how it ended, how long it takes to review, what Claude did, whether the tests passed, and how much changed. The conversation is folded under it: tap **Show the conversation** to read it.

In a session that runs in Switchboard, the chips under the message box show its model, effort and permission mode. Tap one to change it. The effort chip only shows for a model that takes an effort; **Default** is the model's own. From a phone you can pick **Ask before edits**, **Accept edits**, **Plan mode** or **Auto mode**.

To send images or files with a message, tap the paperclip button next to the message box: your phone offers its camera and its photos, and a computer offers its files. In a browser on a computer, you can also paste a screenshot or drop files on the message box. Each shows above the box, with a button to remove it. A message takes up to four images and four files, and about 2.7 MB in all. Your phone makes big photos smaller before it sends them. New session takes them too, and starts the session with them. Sending files, and images with a new session, needs this version of Switchboard on your computer. Queue for later doesn't take attachments.

## Ask about this session

The blue question mark in a session's header opens [Ask about this session](/sessions/ask/), as on your computer: ask Claude what changed, which files it touched or why it took an approach, while it keeps working. Nothing is added to the conversation. On a phone it opens as a sheet, in a wide window on the right, in the place of **Changes**.

A new thread starts with four questions to tap. Follow-ups go with the earlier questions of their thread. **New thread** starts another, and **⋯** lists the earlier ones. The questions you asked on your computer are there too. Under an answer, **Copy** copies it and **Quote in message** puts it in the message box as a quote. Close Ask while Claude answers, and a blue dot on the button says when the answer is ready.

## Changes and Commit

The file button in a session's header, and **Changes** on its catch-up, open what changed in the session's folder (its worktree when it has one): each file with the lines added and removed, and the diff of the file you tap. Swipe it down to close it.

- **Ask Claude to fix…** puts the file's name in the message box, so you only say what to change.
- **Commit** commits every change, with your message. It starts with what you asked Claude. It only commits: nothing is pushed. When a commit hook says no, the phone shows what it printed.

## New session

Pick a project from the row of tiles (swipe for more), then type what Claude should do. On a phone, **All projects** lists every project, with a filter when you have many. Under the box, chips show the project's defaults: the model, the effort, the permission mode, and **This folder** or **New worktree**. Tap one to change it for this session only. On a phone, its choices open from the bottom of the screen.

With **This folder**, the branch chip shows the branch checked out in the project's folder. Pick another local branch and Switchboard checks it out before the session starts. Git keeps your uncommitted changes, or refuses when they conflict; then the session doesn't start and the app says why. A branch checked out in another worktree isn't listed.

With [Auto model](/new-session/auto-model/) on in Settings › Conversation on your computer, the model list starts with **Auto**: Haiku reads your prompt and picks the model the session starts on, from the models you allowed. In the session, the model chip then says what it picked, such as **Auto · Sonnet**, until you pick another model. With Auto off, it isn't offered, and a project whose default is Auto starts on your default model.

With more than one Claude profile, the first chip is the profile the session runs with: the project's own, else your default profile. Pick another for this session only. The other chips then show that profile's defaults, where the project sets none.

While a session works in that project, **Queue for later** puts the prompt in Switchboard's queue instead. Start it from the queue on your computer.

On a phone, while you type, the tiles and the chips make room: only the project's name, the box and the buttons stay, with **Start session** right above the keyboard. Tap the keyboard button next to it to hide the keyboard and see them again.

## On a computer or a tablet

In a wide window, such as the browser of another computer or an iPad, your sessions stay on the left, as in Switchboard. A phone held sideways keeps the phone's layout. Prompts are rows in **Needs you**, with what they ask ("Allow Bash", "a question"). **New session** and the **⋯** menu are at the bottom of the list. The house button next to **Sessions** closes the open session and shows Home on the right.

On a narrower screen, such as an iPad held upright, the sessions can fold away to leave the session the whole width: tap the button at the top left of a session, or press <Kbd keys="mod+b" />. Folded, that button opens them as a drawer, as on a phone. Its **Home** row shows Home on the right. The button next to the computer's name in the drawer, or <Kbd keys="mod+b" /> again, keeps them open on the left. Your choice is kept in this browser.

### What needs you

With no session open, the right side shows what needs you: how many sessions need you, how many are working and how many finished today. Under it, every prompt is a card, the oldest first, with everything you need to answer it there: **Deny**, **Always allow** when it's offered, and **Allow** for a command or an edit, the options of a question with **Skip** and **Answer**, and **Keep planning** and **Start** for a plan. **Open** opens its session.

Below the cards, **Finished, ready to review** shows the sessions that finished and you haven't read, each with how long it takes to review, what Claude did, the tests, **Changes** and **Open**. Then each Claude profile's full usage card, with when each window resets.

### Keys

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <Kbd keys="mod+k" /> | Search your sessions and projects, and run an action. Works in the message box too. |
| <Kbd keys="mod+shift+u" /> or <Kbd keys="u" /> | Open the next session that needs you: the ones waiting for you first, the longest waiting first, then the ones you haven't read. |
| <Kbd keys="j" /> and <Kbd keys="k" /> | Move to the next or previous card, or session in the list. |
| <Kbd keys="a" /> | Allow the card you're on, or in a session the prompt above the message box. |
| <Kbd keys="d" /> | Deny it. |
| <Kbd keys="enter" /> | Open the card you're on. |
| <Kbd keys="n" /> | New session. |
| <Kbd keys="arrowup" /> and <Kbd keys="arrowdown" /> | Move through the files in Changes. |
| <Kbd keys="escape" /> | Close the search, a sheet or Changes, in that order. |
| <Kbd keys="?" /> | Show these keys. |

The letters only work when you're not typing in a field, and never with another key held. <Kbd keys="a" /> does nothing when the prompt is scrolled out of view, so you never allow something you can't see.

Microsoft Edge keeps <Kbd keys="mod+shift+u" /> for Read aloud: use <Kbd keys="u" /> there.
</OnlyOn>

### Search and jump

<Kbd keys="mod+k" />, or **Search or jump to…** at the top of the list, opens the search. Type a few letters of a session's title, its project or its branch. Sessions that need you come first. Under them, **New session in** a project that matches, and actions: **Next that needs you**, **New session**, **Go to Home**, **Turn on notifications**, **Mark all as read**, **Switch to** each of your other computers, **Pair another computer** and **Forget this computer**. Use the arrow keys and <Kbd keys="enter" /> to pick one.

### A session and its Changes

The conversation keeps a comfortable reading width in the middle. The header shows the model and the permission mode (such as **Opus · Auto**), **Ask**, **Changes** with the number of files, and **Stop** while Claude works. The buttons of a prompt show their keys.

In a window at least 1280 pixels wide, **Changes** opens on the right instead of over the conversation: the files, the diff of the one you pick, a commit message that starts with what you asked, **Ask Claude to fix…** and **Commit**. The browser remembers whether you had it open. **Ask** opens in the same place; close it and Changes comes back. Esc closes either.

### The browser tab

The tab's title starts with the number of sessions that need you, such as **(2) Switchboard Remote**, and its icon gets a pink dot. In a session the title is the session's, so you can find the tab among others.

## Notifications

Your phone can tell you when a session needs you or finishes, also when the page is closed. After you pair, a card offers **Turn on** (in a wide window, a strip above the sessions does). Later, open **Notifications** in the **⋯** menu and turn **Needs you** and **Finished** on or off. Nothing comes while Switchboard Remote is open in front of you.

<Aside type="note" title="On an iPhone or iPad">
Notifications only work when Switchboard Remote is on your Home Screen. In Safari, tap Share, then **Add to Home Screen**. Open it from there and turn notifications on. iPhones don't show buttons on notifications: tap one to open the session.
</Aside>

A notification goes from your computer to your browser's push service (Apple's, Google's, Mozilla's or Microsoft's), and from there to your phone. It is encrypted for your phone, so the push service can't read it. Your computer needs to reach those services over HTTPS: behind a strict proxy, notifications may not arrive.

With more than one computer, every computer sends you notifications, and each one starts with the computer's name, such as *mystic · tideline · Allow Bash: npm publish*. Only the computer on screen holds them back while the app is open in front of you. Tap a notification to open its session on the right computer. A computer with a version of Switchboard from before this one can't send notifications next to other computers: **Notifications** names it, and updating Switchboard there fixes it.

Tap a notification to open its session. In Chrome, Edge and Firefox on a computer, a notification about a command or an edit also has **Deny** and **Allow**. They open Switchboard Remote, which answers once it's connected, and says **Already answered** if you, or someone else, answered first.

## Other computers

Switchboard on another computer can pair with this one as a phone does, and show its sessions next to yours. Claude keeps running on the computer that has the session; you follow and steer it from the other one.

1. On the computer that runs the sessions, turn Remote on, click **Pair a device**, then **Copy link**.
2. On the other computer, open Settings → Remote (or *Connect to another computer…* in the command palette), paste the link under **Other computers** and click **Connect**.
3. Both computers show the same 6 digits. Click **Allow** on the first one if they match.

### Its sessions in your sidebar

<Screenshot name="computers-session" alt="A session on the computer mystic open in the normal view: a violet line along the top, on mystic in the header, the terminal and Changes buttons greyed out, tool calls folded into one line, an Allow Bash? card above the message box, and in the sidebar the remote sessions with a mystic chip and the Computers strip with This Mac and mystic" />

The other computer's sessions sit in your sidebar, in the same groups as yours: **Needs you**, **Working**, **Today** and so on. Violet marks everything on another computer, and nothing else uses it:

- the project icon has a small monitor badge;
- the second line of the row ends with a chip with the computer's name, such as **mystic**.

Click a row to open the session in the normal view. A violet line runs along the top, and the header says **on mystic**. It opens in a pane like your own sessions, so you can put it next to one of yours or next to another computer's: see [Split view](/sessions/split-view/). Read the conversation as it happens, answer what Claude asks with the same keys as on your own sessions, send a message (it waits in Up next while Claude works), and **Stop** a turn. When the turn ended but a background command or agent still runs there, the end of the conversation says so in green, and a green pill above the message box lists each task with a **Stop** for it, as on your own sessions. Runs of tool calls fold into one line, such as *Read 4 files · Edited ContentOverview.tsx +42 −6*. Click it to see each step.

While the session runs in Switchboard there, the chips under the message box show its model, effort and permission mode. Click one to change it on that computer. The effort chip only shows for a model that takes an effort, and you can pick **Ask before edits**, **Accept edits**, **Plan mode** or **Auto mode**. With more than one Claude profile there, the profile the session runs with shows next to the computer's name. A running session keeps its profile.

What only works on that computer stays in its place, but is off: **Changes**, the terminal, opening the folder in your editor, pull requests and file links. Point at one to see why, for example *Terminal is on mystic. Not available from here.* Nothing in another computer's conversation runs on yours: only web links open.

Right-click a remote session for **Open**, **Open beside**, **Pin**, **Archive**, **Copy title** and **New session in** its project. Rename and delete it on the computer it runs on.

### Send images and files

To send images or files with a message, click the paperclip button under the message box, or paste or drop them on it. Images show as thumbnails and files as chips with their size, each with a button to remove it. A message still needs some text.

- A message takes up to four images and four files, and about 2.7 MB in all. Big images are made smaller first, as on a phone.
- Files are saved on the other computer, in Switchboard's data folder, and the message mentions each one by its path there, so Claude reads it itself. Switchboard removes them after 30 days.
- An older Switchboard on the other computer only takes images in a session, and nothing in New session. Point at the paperclip to see what it takes. Update Switchboard there to send files too.
- Folders can't be sent. Drop the files in a folder instead.

### Filter by computer

The **Computers** strip at the bottom of the sidebar has a chip for <Os mac="This Mac" windows="This PC" /> and one for each paired computer, with a green dot when it is connected and, in pink, how many of its sessions need you. Click a chip to show only that computer's sessions. Click it again to show all of them. In the project menu (**All projects ▾**), **Show sessions from** has a checkbox for each computer, and the other computers' projects are listed with their chip. The command palette has **Show only mystic** for each connected computer. Each window keeps its own filter.

**Search sessions** at the top of the sidebar finds them by title, project, branch or computer name. Go to session in the command palette (<Kbd shortcut="palette.goto" />) and <Kbd shortcut="session.next-needs-you" /> include them too.

### Start a session there

In New session, **Run on** picks the computer. Its projects become the tiles, and **Start on mystic** starts the session there. See [Run on another computer](/new-session/form/#run-on-another-computer). **New session on mystic…** in the command palette opens it with that computer picked.

With [Clone from GitHub](/projects/clone-from-github/#on-another-computer) on here, **Clone from GitHub…** clones a repository on that computer, into its own **Clone into** folder, adds it as a project there and starts the session in it. Clone from GitHub has to be on in Settings › Projects on that computer too, and it needs this version of Switchboard there.

### Its tokens in Usage

The [Usage view](/usage/#tokens-on-other-computers) adds up the tokens your claude.ai account used on the computers you paired, next to this one's. **All computers** in its header turns it on, and the breakdown marks the other computers' sessions with their name.

### When a computer is offline

Its sessions stay in the sidebar, dimmed, with when it was last seen: *mystic is offline. Last seen 2h ago.* An open session keeps its last messages under a banner, *mystic is offline. Trying again in 30s.*, with **Try again**. The message box is off until the computer is back. A computer you remove takes its sessions with it.

### Reconnect or remove

When the computer's tunnel gets a new address, click **Reconnect a paired device** on it, **Copy link**, and paste that link under **Other computers** too. **Open** shows a computer's page, and **Remove** takes it off this list. To stop a computer from reaching yours, **Revoke** it under **Paired devices** on yours.

## When your phone can't connect

A line under the header says why, when your phone can tell:

- **Its tunnel address changed**: a Cloudflare quick tunnel got a new address. Click **Reconnect a paired device phones** in Settings → Remote and scan that code, or use a tunnel whose address stays the same.
- **Is it asleep or offline?**: nothing answers. Wake your computer, or check its network.
- **Is Switchboard running, with Remote on?**: the address answers, but Switchboard doesn't.

You chose **Disconnect**? Then nothing connects until you tap **Connect** (see [Your computer's menu](#your-computers-menu)).

**Help** explains what to do, with a link to this page. While it's offline, your phone keeps showing the last list, greyed out, and the buttons that need your computer are off. It tries again by itself, and at once when you come back to the page.

## Updates

The remote app comes from Switchboard's website, so it updates without the App Store. On an iPhone, the app on your Home Screen keeps running the version it started with until iOS closes it. So it checks for a new version when you open it, each time you come back to it, and every 30 minutes while it's open.

When there is one, a blue bar says **A new version is ready.** **Reload** loads it and brings you back to the screen you were on. **Later** hides the bar for an hour. Meanwhile, pulling your sessions down says **Release to update** and loads the new version too.

## Good to know

- Your computer has to be on and Switchboard running. Sleep stops Remote until the computer wakes up.
- The phone only lists the sessions your sidebar lists, outside Archived, and can only open those.
- Messages you send from your phone show **From *your phone*** under them on the phone.
- Scrolled up in a conversation, **Jump to latest** takes you back down. It says **New messages** when something arrived below.
- The chip and the offline help use your computer's name, which you set in <Os mac="System Settings → General → About → Name" windows="Settings → System → About → Rename this PC" />.
- Pairing is closed after five wrong attempts, until you click **Pair a device** again.
- Your phone keeps its key in the browser, for this site only. Someone with your unlocked phone can use Remote until you revoke it.
