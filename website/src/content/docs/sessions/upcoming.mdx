Upcoming shows what is going to run, across all your projects: everything in the [queue](/new-session/queue/), the scheduled runs of the next 7 days, every [schedule](/new-session/schedules/), and every [trigger](/projects/triggers/). **History** shows what ran in the last 7 days. Use it to see your day and week at a glance, to reorder the queue, or to skip a single run without pausing its schedule.

## Open it

- Click **Upcoming** under the search field in the sidebar. In the minimal sidebar, click the calendar icon under Home.
- Press <Kbd shortcut="upcoming" />.
- In the command palette, choose *Go to Upcoming*. *Queue…* opens it on the queue, and *Schedules…* on the schedules.
- Click **See all in Upcoming** on the Queue or Schedules card on [Home](/sessions/home/), or **All projects in Upcoming** under Schedules in a project's [Sessions tab](/projects/sessions/).
- Click a notification that says a scheduled run is in the queue, was skipped or didn't start. Upcoming opens with that schedule's run in view.

The **Upcoming** row in the sidebar shows a count: your queued items plus your active schedules. Before the count it says what matters most:

- "1 ready", in green, when a queued item is ready to start;
- otherwise the next scheduled run, such as "next 08:00", or "next Tue 08:00" when it isn't today;
- "nothing planned" when nothing is queued and you have no schedules.

## Filter

At the top:

- **All**, **Queue**, **Schedules** and **Triggers** pick what shows. **Queue** shows only the queue, across the full width. **Schedules** shows the scheduled runs and the schedules side by side. **Triggers** shows only the triggers.
- **All projects** filters by project. Its menu lists each project that has something queued or scheduled, or a trigger, with the counts as "queued · schedules".

Each window remembers its own filters.

**Add to queue** and **Schedule a session** open [New session](/new-session/form/) to write the prompt. With **Schedule a session**, Start's main action is **Schedule…**, so <Kbd keys="enter" /> opens the schedule dialog instead of starting a session.

## Queue

The **Queue** card lists your queued prompts in the order they start. Each row has its place, the project, the prompt's first line and what it waits for, such as "Starts after "Fix transcript scroll jump"" or "Waits for "Slide transitions for scenes"".

- **Start now** starts it right away. On a ready item the button is **Start**.
- **⋯** opens the same menu as in the sidebar: **Edit in New session…**, **Wait for…**, **Start by itself**, the move items and **Remove**.
- Drag a row by its grip, or press <Kbd shortcut="queue.move" />, to change the order.

See [Queue](/new-session/queue/) for how queued items start.

## Scheduled runs

The **Scheduled runs** card lists every run still to come in the next 7 days, grouped by day: "Today · Mon 19 Oct", "Tomorrow · Tue 20 Oct", then the days after. Each run shows its time, the project and the schedule's name. Paused schedules have no runs here.

When two or more days in a row have exactly the same runs as the day above them, they fold into one line, such as "Thu 22 Oct · Fri 23 Oct" with "2× Review open pull requests and Standup summary, each day". Click it to see each day.

### Skip a run

Point at a run that is still to come, or move the focus to it, to show **Run now**, **Skip this run** and **⋯**.

- **Run now** starts a run straight away. The run at its time still happens.
- **Skip this run** skips this one run. The schedule stays active and runs again at its next time. The row is dimmed and says **Skipped by you**, with **Undo**. A message in the corner says which run you skipped, such as "Skipped Tue 20 Oct 08:00 of Review open pull requests.", with **Undo** too.
- **⋯** has **Run now**, **Skip this run**, **Edit schedule…** and **Pause schedule** (or **Resume schedule**).

The menu of a schedule on Home, in a project's Sessions tab and here has **Skip next run**, which does the same for the schedule's next run.

## Schedules

The **Schedules** card lists every schedule, with its rule and project. The switch on each row pauses and resumes it: on means active. A paused schedule is dimmed and says **Paused**, or **Folder not found** when Seshboard paused it because its folder is gone. A schedule set to **Once** leaves the list once it has run; its run shows under [History](#history).

**⋯** has the schedule's menu: **Run now**, **Skip next run**, **Edit…**, **Pause** or **Resume**, and **Delete**, which has **Undo**.

Under the card, a line says when schedules run: <Os mac="while Seshboard is running, also with its windows closed" windows="while a Seshboard window is open" />.

## Triggers

The **Triggers** card lists every [trigger](/projects/triggers/) across your projects, with its project, its last check, how many of its sessions are busy, how many events wait and how many were skipped, and its number of events: "checked 2m ago · 2 of 3 busy · 0 waiting". A click opens the project's Triggers tab.

A paused trigger says why, such as "Paused after 5 failed checks: gh is signed out.", with **Resume**.

Under the triggers, **Waiting for a slot** lists the events that wait, oldest first, each with **Start now**.

## History

The **History** card, under the schedules, lists the runs of the last 7 days, newest first: each with when it ran ("Today 13:58", "Yesterday 08:00"), the schedule and project, and how it went.

| It says | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Finished**, with **Open** | The run started a session. **Open** opens it. While the session is still going it says **Working** or **Waiting for you**. |
| **Skipped: the last run was still working** | The session of the run before was still busy. |
| **Missed** | Seshboard was closed at its time, and the schedule is set to **Skip it**. |
| **In the queue** | It went to the queue at the [focus limit](/focus-limit/) or a usage limit. |
| **Skipped by you** | You skipped it with **Skip this run**. |
| **Didn't start** | It couldn't start, with the reason, such as "Folder not found". |

A schedule set to **Once** only shows here after its run, for 7 days. After that Seshboard removes it. The session it started keeps the repeat icon in the sidebar.

## Nothing planned

With nothing queued and no schedules, Upcoming says *Nothing planned*, with **Add to queue** and **Schedule a session**.
