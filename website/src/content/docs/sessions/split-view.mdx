Split view tiles your sessions like a tiling window manager. Open as many as fit, side by side, one above the other, or mixed, so you can follow one while you work in another, or compare what several sessions did. Each pane is a full session view, with its own message box, Changes and terminal.

## Open a pane

With a session open, open another one next to it:

- <Kbd shortcut="sidebar.open-beside" /> a session in the sidebar.
- Right-click a session in the sidebar and choose **Open beside**.
- In <Kbd shortcut="palette.goto" />, pick a session with <Kbd keys="alt+enter" />.
- Drag a session from the sidebar onto a pane (see below).

The new session splits the active pane. A pane that is at least as wide as it is tall gets the new session on its right; a taller pane gets it below. If that doesn't fit, the other way is tried.

Every pane needs at least 340 pixels each way, so the number of panes depends on the size of the window. When there is no room for another pane, Switchboard says so and opens nothing. Make the window larger, or close a pane first.

## How to use it

- **The active pane** has a yellow line along the top of its header; the others are a little faded. Click in a pane to make it active. Keyboard shortcuts, such as <Kbd shortcut="terminal.toggle" first /> or <Kbd shortcut="find" />, work on the active pane.
- **Picking a session** in the sidebar opens it in the active pane. If it is already open in another pane, that pane becomes active instead.
- **In the sidebar**, sessions in the other panes have a light outline, and the active one the usual selection.
- Only the pane in the top left corner has the sidebar button in its header.
- In a narrow pane, headers and bars get more compact: the message box chips show fewer words, and project actions show only their icon.

## Drag panes into place

Drag a pane by its title in the header (the project and session name, which shows a hand when you point at it), or a session from the sidebar (the list or the narrow rail), into the panes. Anywhere over them is a place to drop, and a frame shows the region the tile will take before you let go.

- **A window edge.** A band along each edge of the panes (a faint dashed strip while you drag) docks the tile across the whole length of that edge: a column the full height on the left or right, a row the full width on the top or bottom. It beats the edge of whichever pane lies under it. With a layout of one pane next to two stacked ones, dragging the lower one to the right edge gives three equal columns. When the layout already runs that way, the tile joins it; otherwise the whole layout becomes one cell and the tile the other.
- **A pane's edge** (left, right, top or bottom) docks the tile on that side of that pane. In a row of panes it becomes another pane in the row, not a nested split.
- **A pane's middle** swaps the two panes. A session from the sidebar that is not open yet takes the place of the pane's session there; the session it replaced stays in the sidebar.

A session that is already open is never opened twice: dragging its row in the sidebar moves its tile, exactly like dragging that tile's header to the same place.

Release to drop, or press <Kbd keys="escape" /> to cancel. The buttons in the header keep working as usual. If the result would not fit in the window, the frame turns red and says so, and releasing changes nothing.

Sizes follow the same rule as VS Code. If the panes beside each other were all the same size, they are all the same size again once one is added or taken away. Otherwise a new pane takes half of the pane it docks beside, and a pane that leaves gives its space to the pane next to it.

Nothing reloads when you rearrange panes: the message you were typing, the scroll position and Find stay where you left them.

## Resize and turn

- A **divider** sits between every two neighbouring panes. Drag it to resize them. It snaps to equal sizes, and no pane gets smaller than its minimum.
- Click a divider, or tab to it, and use the arrow keys (left and right for side by side, up and down for stacked). <Kbd keys="home" /> and <Kbd keys="end" /> go as far as the minimum sizes allow.
- **Double-click** a divider to make the panes around it equal.
- <Kbd shortcut="pane.orientation" /> turns the panes the active pane sits in the other way: side by side become stacked, and back. The header has a button for it too (hidden in a narrow pane), and the command palette has *Stack panes* and *Show panes side by side*. If the turned panes would not fit, nothing changes.
- <Kbd shortcut="pane.swap" /> swaps the active pane with the next one.

## Move between panes

- <Kbd shortcut="pane.focus-left" />, <Kbd shortcut="pane.focus-right" />, <Kbd shortcut="pane.focus-up" /> and <Kbd shortcut="pane.focus-down" /> go to the pane next door.
- *Focus next pane* in the command palette goes through them in reading order.

## When the window is too small

If you make the window smaller than the panes need, Switchboard shows only the active pane, with a note above it. The other panes stay open, and they all come back, as you had them, when the window is large enough again. Press one of the keys above to show another pane in the meantime.

## Close a pane

- The **×** in a pane's header (*Close this pane*), <Kbd shortcut="pane.close" /> or the command palette closes that pane. The panes next to it take its space.
- <Kbd shortcut="pane.close-other" /> closes every pane but the active one.

Closing a pane only closes the view: a session that is working keeps running. Archiving or deleting a session that is open in a pane closes that pane too.

<Aside type="tip">
<Kbd shortcut="home" /> closes all the panes and goes to Home.
</Aside>

## Settings

Split view has no settings. The panes are not saved: after a restart Switchboard opens the session you had open last.
