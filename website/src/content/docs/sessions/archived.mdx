Sessions you haven't touched for a while move to **Archived**, out of the way, so the sidebar shows what matters now. Nothing is deleted: an archived session comes back as soon as there is something new, and you can open, continue or unarchive it at any time.

<Screenshot name="archived" alt="The sidebar with the Archived section opened under the other sessions, its sessions slightly faded" width={480} eager />

## Open it

While it is closed, **Archived** is docked at the bottom of the sidebar with its count. Click it to open it: it slides up right under your other sessions. Click its header again to close it. With the header focused, → opens it and ← closes it.

While you type in **Search sessions**, archived sessions that match show under the rest, even with Archived closed.

## What gets archived

**On its own.** A finished session moves to Archived after 3 days without activity. Choose the number of days in [Settings › Sessions](/settings/sessions/). It stays in the main list while it is:

- working, or waiting for you;
- failed;
- finished and unread;
- open in Switchboard;
- pinned.

**By you.** Archive a session yourself to put it away now:

- Right-click it and choose **Archive** (until new activity).
- Middle-click a finished session (<Kbd shortcut="sidebar.archive" />). Sessions that are working, waiting for you or failed stay where they are.
- With the session open, choose *Archive* in the command palette.
- Select several sessions with <Kbd shortcut="sidebar.select" first /> and choose **Archive** in the bar at the bottom of the sidebar, or right-click one of them.

You can archive a session even while Claude is working on it. It stays archived while it works, and comes back when it has finished, so you see the result. A session that <Pill tone="needs-you">needs you</Pill> or failed always shows in the main list.

Archiving a session unpins it. Archiving the session you have open closes it; with two sessions side by side, the other one takes the full width.

<Aside type="note">
If the session has an [unsent message](/sessions/unsent-drafts/), Switchboard asks first (*Archive and discard the unsent message?*), because archiving discards it.
</Aside>

## Bring a session back

- **New activity** brings it back by itself: when you send it a message, or it finishes something new.
- **Unarchive** it from its menu, from the command palette, or with several selected (**Unarchive** in the bar at the bottom). An unarchived session stays in the main list until it has new activity, however long it was quiet. After that, it's archived again once it has been quiet for the days you set.
- **Select all** on the Archived header (while selecting) picks every archived session at once.

## Manage archived sessions

Click the **⋯** on the Archived header:

- **Manage sessions…** opens [Settings › Sessions](/settings/sessions/) with only archived sessions listed, grouped by month, to unarchive them or move many to the <Os mac="Trash" windows="Recycle Bin" /> at once.
- **Archive settings…** opens the same page on the number of days.

## Delete a session

To remove a session for good, delete it:

- Right-click it and choose **Delete session…**, or press <Kbd shortcut="sidebar.delete" /> in the sidebar.
- With several selected, choose **Delete…** in the bar at the bottom.
- With the session open, choose *Delete session…* in the command palette.

After you confirm with **Move to <Os mac="Trash" windows="Recycle Bin" />**, the conversation and any subagent transcripts go to the <Os mac="Trash" windows="Recycle Bin" />, so you can still get them back from there.

## Delete old archived sessions

Archived sessions stay on disk until you delete them. To have Switchboard move them to the <Os mac="Trash" windows="Recycle Bin" /> after some time, turn on **Delete archived sessions** in [Settings › Sessions](/settings/sessions/#delete-archived-sessions). It is off by default and asks first.

Claude Code also deletes old transcripts by itself, after 30 days unless you changed `cleanupPeriodDays`. [Settings › Sessions](/settings/sessions/#claude-codes-own-cleanup) shows what each Claude profile uses and lets you change it.

## Settings

[Settings › Sessions](/settings/sessions/) sets when sessions are archived and deleted. [Settings, Backup](/settings/#backup) can include your archived sessions in an export when you tick them.
