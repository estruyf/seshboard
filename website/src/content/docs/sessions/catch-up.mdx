When a session finishes a turn, Switchboard puts a short briefing over the conversation: what you asked, what Claude did, what it skipped, and the one thing it needs from you next. It comes with the signals you would otherwise look up yourself: did the tests pass, how much changed, and did Claude stay inside what you asked for.

<Screenshot name="catch-up-banner" alt="The catch-up banner above the conversation: Finished, the ask, about 3 minutes to review, Commit and Details; under it Commit the changes in green, why, and the chips 14 tests passed, +120 −40 · 6 files and Followed the plan" eager />

## When it shows

A session gets a catch-up when Claude finishes a turn, or stops with an error. Your next message clears it, and so does Claude going back to work. A session that is working has no catch-up.

A session that waits for a permission, a question or a plan to approve has none either: the card above the message box asks, as always. Its **Why?** button tells you why Claude asks. See [Permissions](/permissions/#why-is-claude-asking).

The banner shows when there is something to decide: the session finished and you haven't read it, or it stopped with an error. Once you've read a session, the catch-up folds into one line at the top, so it is one key away and never in the way.

<Screenshot name="catch-up-strip" alt="The folded catch-up: Finished, Commit the changes, about 3 minutes to review, a Commit button and Show catch-up with Space" width={760} />

Opening a session with a catch-up doesn't mark it as read by itself. **Mark as read**, or anything that ends what it needs, does. A catch-up made while you were looking at the session (you watched it finish) is no news, so that session is read as usual.

## What the banner says

- **The first line:** the state (**Finished** in blue, or **Finished with errors** in red), your ask, how long it takes to look it over ("~2 min to review"), the main action and **Details**.
- **Your ask** is the prompt that started this piece of work. When your last message only answered Claude ("yes, go on"), it is the one before.
- **The second line:** the one thing it needs from you, in its colour (commit in green, reply in red, review in orange, or nothing), why, and the chips.
- **The chips:** the tests (**14 tests passed**, **2 of 9 tests fail** or **No tests run**), the changes (**+120 −40 · 6 files**), files outside what you asked for, and whether Claude followed its plan.

**Details** (<Kbd shortcut="catchup.expand" />) opens the rest: **What it did** and **Skipped or not finished**, a few plain lines each, and the other actions.

<Screenshot name="catch-up-banner-details" alt="The banner with Details open: What it did and Skipped or not finished, then Review changes, Reply and Archive" width={760} />

### What it needs from you

| It needs | When | Main action |
|---|---|---|
| **Commit** | It finished cleanly and changed files. | **Commit** (<Kbd shortcut="catchup.commit" />) |
| **Reply** | Tests fail, it stopped with an error, or it asked you something. | **Reply** (<Kbd shortcut="catchup.reply" />) |
| **Review** | It changed files outside the plan, or outside what you asked for. | **Review changes** (<Kbd shortcut="catchup.review" />) |
| **Nothing** | It finished cleanly without changing files. | **Archive** (<Kbd shortcut="catchup.archive" />) |

<Kbd shortcut="catchup.primary" /> always runs the main action.

### The signals

The signals come from what Claude ran and from git, never from a model:

- **Tests:** the last test command of the turn (`npm test`, `npm run check`, `vitest`, `jest`, `pytest`, `go test`, `cargo test`, `dotnet test` and the like), and whether it passed. The counts show when the output has them.
- **Changes:** what changed in the session's checkout (or worktree) against the last commit, plus the new files Claude made.
- **Outside the plan:** with a plan, the files and folders it names; without one, the paths your prompt names. A file Claude changed outside those shows here. A file outside the project or worktree always does. When neither the plan nor your prompt names any paths, the chip doesn't show.
- **The plan:** **Followed the plan**, or **Deviated from the plan**, only when the session had one.

The review time adds up the lines and files that changed, failing tests and files outside the plan.

## The actions

Each button shows its key. The keys work while the catch-up is on screen and you're not typing in the message box or another field.

| Action | Key | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Review changes** | <Kbd shortcut="catchup.review" /> | Opens the [Changes panel](/terminal-changes-git/changes/), or closes it (**Hide changes**). |
| **Commit** | <Kbd shortcut="catchup.commit" /> | Opens the Commit dialog. Once it went through, the banner says **Committed** and offers **Archive**. |
| **Reply** | <Kbd shortcut="catchup.reply" /> | Puts you in the message box, which says "Reply to Claude about …". |
| **Mark as read** | <Kbd shortcut="catchup.read" /> | Marks the session read. The [focus limit](/focus-limit/)'s count drops right away. |
| **Archive** | <Kbd shortcut="catchup.archive" /> | Archives the session and opens the next one in the sidebar's order. |

<Kbd shortcut="catchup.close" /> closes one thing at a time: the message box you went to reply in, then the Changes panel, then Details, then it folds the banner. In the folded line, **Show catch-up** (<Kbd shortcut="catchup.expand" />) opens it again.

## Quickest to unblock

The sidebar can sort sessions by what takes least to unblock. Choose **Quickest to unblock** at the top of the sidebar, next to **Recent**. Switchboard remembers your choice.

<Screenshot name="sidebar-quickest" alt="The sidebar sorted by Quickest to unblock: Needs review · quickest first, then Read, with review times and a warning on a session that changed a file outside the plan, then Still working" width={360} />

- **Needs review · quickest first:** sessions that wait for you, then finished ones you haven't read, each by how long they take to review. Failing tests come last among equal times.
- **Read:** sessions you've looked at.
- **Still working:** sessions Claude is working on.

The Queue and Pinned only show in **Recent**. In both orders, a session with a catch-up says how long it takes on its second line ("acme-store · ~2 min to review", or "needs you · ~1 min"), and a warning shows when Claude changed files outside the plan. In the [minimal rail](/sessions/sidebar/), the warning is a small badge on the icon.

<Kbd shortcut="session.next-needs-you" /> goes to the next session that needs you in this order, whichever order the sidebar shows, and wraps around.

## Settings

In [Settings](/settings/), under **Conversation**, **Catch-up**, **Write catch-up summaries with Haiku** turns the catch-up on and off. It is on by default.

Off, no banner shows and Haiku is never called. You can still ask for one: run **Catch up on this session** from the [command palette](/command-palette/), and its lines come from the tool calls ("Edited 6 files", "Ran npm run check").

### What Haiku sees

Haiku gets your prompt, Claude's last reply, a list of the tool calls (the tool, the file or command, and how it ended), the signals and the plan. It never sees what is in your files, a diff or your memory. Haiku has a few seconds; if it doesn't answer in time, the banner is written without it: **What it did** starts with the opening sentence and the bold headings of Claude's last reply, followed by the tool calls ("Edited 6 files", "Ran npm run check").

Each summary uses a few thousand Haiku tokens on the session's Claude profile, so it counts toward that plan. In [Usage](/usage/), they show as their own row, **Catch-up summaries**. The answers to **Why?** on a permission count there too.

<Aside>
Sessions that run in a terminal or another app get no catch-up by themselves. For one of those, run **Catch up on this session** from the [command palette](/command-palette/) once it has finished.
</Aside>
