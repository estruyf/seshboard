AI makes starting work easy, and every session you start is one more thing to keep up with. The focus limit helps you not start more than you can follow: you choose how many sessions you want going at once, and Seshboard asks before you go over. Why it helps: [The AI chaos beast in your head](https://www.eliostruyf.com/ai-chaos-beast-head/).

<Screenshot name="focus" alt="The focus limit counter in the sidebar footer showing 2 / 3, with its popover listing the sessions that count, each with its state and an Open button" eager />

## Open it

- Turn it on in [Settings → Focus](/settings/#focus) with the **Focus limit** switch.
- In the command palette: *Focus limit: turn on*, *Focus limit: turn off* and *Focus limit: set limit…* (pick from 1 session to 10 sessions).
- **Change limit** in the note New session shows at the limit opens Settings → Focus.

<Screenshot name="settings-focus" alt="Settings on the Focus section: the Focus limit switch, Sessions at the same time with minus and plus buttons, When you reach it with Nudge and Strict, Count terminal and IDE sessions, and Count sessions on other computers" width={900} />

## What counts

A session counts while it's on your mind:

- Claude is <Pill tone="working">Working</Pill> on it;
- it <Pill tone="needs-you">Needs you</Pill>: a permission or a question;
- it finished something you haven't read yet (<Pill tone="unread">Unread</Pill>).

Reading the result or archiving the session frees its place. Stopped sessions never count. By default only sessions you started or continued in Seshboard count; turn on *Count terminal and IDE sessions* to also count Claude Code running in your terminal or editor. Sessions on computers you paired count too (they are on by default); turn off *Count sessions on other computers* to leave them out. A computer that is offline doesn't count.

## The counter

While the limit is on, a counter at the bottom of the sidebar shows how many sessions are going, such as `2 / 3`:

| Counter | Means |
|---|---|
| Neutral | Under the limit |
| Yellow | At the limit |
| Pink | Over the limit |

Click it to see which sessions count: needs you first, each with its project, its state ("Needs you · 12m", "Working", "Done, not read yet") and **Open**. In the minimal sidebar rail the counter shows as `2/3` and its list opens to the right.

## At the limit

When you are at the limit, [New session](/new-session/form/) shows a note above the message box: "You have 3 of 3 sessions going", with the sessions that count and **Open** for each. Its **Add to queue** keeps your idea in the [queue](/new-session/queue/) instead.

What Start does depends on how strict you set the limit:

- **Nudge** (the default): Start asks first, "Start a 4th session?", with **Add to queue**, **Start anyway** and **Cancel**. The note in New session also has **Start anyway**.
- **Strict**: Start waits. The question says "Finish one first", with **Open** for a session that needs you, **Add to queue** and **Cancel**. Finish, read or archive a session first. In New session, **Add to queue** stays in Start's menu.

<Kbd keys="escape" /> or **Cancel** closes the question and keeps your prompt where it was. The question never starts anything over the limit without you saying so.

## Everything that asks

Every way of starting work goes through the same question:

- starting a session in New session or from the command palette;
- starting a queued item (there the queue button says **Keep it queued**);
- a message that brings back a session that doesn't count right now;
- forking a session;
- a project action that sends Claude a prompt;
- opening Claude in the terminal.

Answering a session that already counts, stopping Claude, archiving, reviewing changes and committing are never held up.

A queued item that [starts by itself](/new-session/queue/#start-by-itself) never asks, since nobody may be there to answer. At the limit it waits, and its row says "waits for a free slot (focus limit)". It starts when a slot frees: when you read, archive or stop a session.

A [scheduled](/new-session/schedules/) run doesn't ask either. At the limit it goes to the queue as an item that starts by itself, and starts when a slot frees. A session a [trigger](/projects/triggers/) starts does the same, on top of the trigger's own **At once** limit.

<Aside>
Turning the limit off and on again keeps the number you chose.
</Aside>

## Settings

In [Settings → Focus](/settings/#focus):

- **Focus limit**: on or off.
- **Sessions at the same time**: 1 to 10, 3 to start with. Working, waiting for you, or finished but not read yet.
- **When you reach it**: **Nudge** asks first. **Strict** waits until you finish, settle or stop one.
- **Count terminal and IDE sessions**: also count live Claude Code sessions started outside Seshboard.
- **Count sessions on other computers** (on by default): also count sessions that are working, need you or are unread on computers you paired.
