**Settings › Notifications** sets how Switchboard gets your attention while you work in another session or app: with a <Os mac="macOS" windows="Windows" /> notification, a sound, or both. You never get either for the session you are looking at in a focused window.

## Open it

- In Settings, choose **Notifications** in the list on the left.
- Run *Settings: Notifications* in the command palette (<Kbd shortcut="palette.commands" />) while Settings is open.

## Show notifications

On by default. A notification shows when Claude needs you (a permission, a question or a plan), when Claude finishes a turn or stops with an error, and when a [project action](/projects/actions/) ends. Click it to open the session. See [Session states](/sessions/states/#notifications-and-the-badge) for what each one says.

Turn it off to stop the notifications. <Os mac="The Dock badge still counts the sessions that wait for you, and the Dock icon still bounces." windows="The dot on the taskbar button still says sessions wait for you, and the button still flashes." />

The command palette has *Notifications: turn off* and *Notifications: turn on*.

## Play sounds

Off by default. With **Play sounds** on, Switchboard plays a sound at the same moments, also with notifications off. There are two sounds:

- **Session complete**: when Claude finishes a turn or stops with an error, and when a project action ends.
- **Needs input**: when Claude asks for a permission, has a question or a plan.

Pick each from the system sounds of your computer, or **None** for no sound. Choosing a sound plays it once; the speaker button next to it plays it again. When several sessions change at the same moment, you hear one sound, and Needs input wins.

**Volume** sets how loud both sounds play. Let go of the slider to hear the new volume.

The command palette has *Sounds: turn on* and *Sounds: turn off*.

<OnlyOn os="mac">

The sounds are the classic macOS alert sounds, such as Glass, Ping and Tink.

</OnlyOn>

<OnlyOn os="windows">

The sounds are the ones in Windows' Media folder, such as Windows Notify Messaging. With **Play sounds** on, the Windows notification itself is silent, so you don't hear two sounds.

</OnlyOn>

<Aside type="tip">
Sounds you pick on one computer move with an [exported backup](/settings/backup/). A sound the other computer doesn't have plays its own default sound instead.
</Aside>
