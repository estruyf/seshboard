**Settings › Notifications** sets how Switchboard gets your attention while you work in another session or app: with a <Os mac="macOS" windows="Windows" /> notification, a sound, or both. Notifications and sounds each have their own card and switch, so you can have one without the other. You never get either for the session you are looking at in a focused window.

<Screenshot name="settings-notifications" alt="Settings on Notifications and sounds: the Notifications card with its switch and Send a test notification, and the Sounds card with a row for Needs you, Finished, Stopped with an error and Project action ended, each with a sound and a play button, and Volume at the bottom" />

## Open it

- In Settings, choose **Notifications** in the list on the left.
- Run *Settings: Notifications* in the command palette (<Kbd shortcut="palette.commands" />) while Settings is open.

## Notifications

On by default. A notification shows when Claude needs you (a permission, a question or a plan), when Claude finishes a turn or stops with an error, and when a [project action](/projects/actions/) ends. Click it to open the session. See [Session states](/sessions/states/#notifications-and-the-badge) for what each one says.

Turn the switch off to stop the notifications. <Os mac="The Dock badge still counts the sessions that wait for you, and the Dock icon still bounces." windows="The dot on the taskbar button still says sessions wait for you, and the button still flashes." />

**Send a test notification** shows one notification like the ones for a session that needs you, so you can check that they come through. It is off while notifications are off.

The command palette has *Notifications: turn off* and *Notifications: turn on*.

### When the system turns them off

<Os mac="macOS" windows="Windows" /> can turn off notifications for an app on its own. When it has, Settings › Notifications says so at the top of the page, with **<Os mac="Open System Settings" windows="Open Windows settings" />** to go and turn them on. Sounds and <Os mac="the Dock badge" windows="the taskbar dot" /> still work. Switchboard checks again when you come back to its window.

## Sounds

Off by default. With **Sounds** on, Switchboard plays a sound for each of these moments, with notifications on or off:

| Moment | When | Sound on a new install |
|---|---|---|
| **Needs you** | Claude asks for a permission, has a question or a plan. | <Os mac="Ping" windows="Windows Notify Messaging" /> |
| **Finished** | Claude finished a turn. | <Os mac="Glass" windows="Windows Notify System Generic" /> |
| **Stopped with an error** | Claude stopped because something failed. | <Os mac="Basso" windows="Windows Critical Stop" /> |
| **Project action ended** | A project action like Dev or Test stopped. | <Os mac="Pop" windows="Windows Ding" /> |

Each moment has a different sound, so you can tell them apart without looking. Pick another from the system sounds of your computer, or **None** for no sound. Move over a sound in the list to hear it. The play button next to it plays the sound you picked. When several sessions change at the same moment, you hear one sound: Needs you first, then Stopped with an error, Finished and Project action ended.

**Volume** sets how loud every sound plays. Let go of the slider to hear the new volume.

Turning **Sounds** off keeps the sounds you picked: they show dimmed, and come back when you turn it on again.

The command palette has *Sounds: turn on* and *Sounds: turn off*.

<OnlyOn os="mac">

The sounds are the classic macOS alert sounds, such as Glass, Ping and Tink.

</OnlyOn>

<OnlyOn os="windows">

The sounds are the ones in Windows' Media folder, such as Windows Notify Messaging. With **Sounds** on, the Windows notification itself is silent, so you don't hear two sounds.

</OnlyOn>

<Aside type="tip">
Sounds you pick on one computer move with an [exported backup](/settings/backup/). A sound the other computer doesn't have plays its own default sound for that moment instead.
</Aside>
